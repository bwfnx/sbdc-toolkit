# Shipley Business Development Process — Quick Reference

Synthesized from Eric Gregory (Shipley/APMP), VisibleThread x Shipley POWeR Guide, and the 2025 GovCon Clarity Report. Adapted for small business use by Brandon Mason, Maryland SBDC.

Read the phase relevant to where the user is — don't load this entire file into working memory unless you need the full picture.

---

## Phase 0 — Market Segmentation (Steps 1-9)

**Purpose:** Pick your lane before you chase anything.

Key steps:
1. Define your offering tied to NAICS codes — if you can't describe what you sell to the government in two sentences, stop here
2. Identify 3-5 target agencies whose missions align with your capabilities
3. Forecast market size using USAspending.gov and GovWin — if total spend in your niche is $200M and you're a $2M company, healthy market; if it's $10M total, may not be enough work
4. Identify competitors — pull the last 10 awards in your NAICS at your target agency; who won and at what price is your competitive landscape
5. Assess qualifications honestly — if every RFP requires a Secret clearance and you don't have facility clearance, that's a gap to close before bidding
6. Validate fit with 3-5 year strategic plan
7. Evaluate business case — a $500K contract costing $50K in B&P with 20% win rate means $250K expected cost per win
8. Make the Marketing/Campaign decision — write it down: "We are pursuing [agency] for [service] because [reason]"
9. Deploy BD resources against chosen segments

**Gate: Campaign Decision** — commit resources or move on.

## Phase 1 — Long-Term Positioning (Steps 10-17)

**Purpose:** Get known before you need to bid.

Key steps:
10. Benchmark capabilities on a 2x2 matrix: relationship strength vs. capability strength
11. Create a scoring checklist to filter opportunities (customer relationship, technical fit, price range, competition level, strategic value)
12. Build relationships — attend industry days, schedule one-on-ones, respond to RFIs. "The best BD activity for a small business is showing up. Industry days are free."
13. Detect trends — read agency strategic plans and Congressional budget justifications. "When an agency publishes a new strategic plan, that's a 3-year roadmap of what they're going to buy."
14. Develop positioning strategy with 3-5 specific 90-day actions
15. Position as subject matter expert — write white papers, present at events. "Not a sales pitch — a genuine analysis with a suggested approach."
16. Target customer visionaries — find program managers and CORs before the RFP drops
17. Make the Interest decision — "Before you spend a dollar, answer honestly: does the customer know your name?"

**Gate: Interest Decision** — is the opportunity real and funded? Does the customer know us?

## Phase 2 — Opportunity Assessment (Steps 18-27)

**Purpose:** Qualify the opportunity before investing capture resources.

Key steps:
18. Confirm opportunity is real and funded — check budget, verify on SAM.gov forecasts. "I had a client spend 6 months positioning for a contract that got canceled because funding was reprogrammed."
19. Understand customer's mission and pain points — they don't care about your ISO certification; they care about resolving help desk tickets in under 4 hours
20. Map requirements to capabilities — identify gaps honestly
21. Cultivate position early — 52% of top performers identify leads 12+ months before the RFP (2025 Clarity Report)
22. Identify probable competitors from FPDS and award notices
23. Build a bidder comparison matrix — score yourself and competitors on likely evaluation criteria. "Be honest. If the incumbent has a 10-year relationship and you've never met the CO, don't score yourself equal."
24. Evaluate B&P resources needed — a $10M opportunity might require $30K-$50K in B&P
25. Identify teaming partners — "Teaming isn't weakness — it's smart strategy. 72% of small businesses cite teaming as their #1 source of new opportunities."
26. Check for organizational conflicts of interest — finding out at proposal time is too late
27. Make the Pursuit decision

**Gate: Pursuit Decision** — do requirements match competencies? Do we have a baseline solution? Have we established a price to compete?

## Phase 3 — Capture Planning (Steps 28-40)

**Purpose:** Develop the win strategy before the RFP drops.

Key steps:
28. Establish capture core team — even a one-person shop needs identified roles (you + proposal consultant + accountant)
29. Write a capture plan — one page minimum: win themes, top 3 competitors, 5 must-do actions before RFP drops
30. Conduct customer profile and hot buttons analysis — "Call the program manager. Ask: 'What are the three biggest challenges in this program?' Those are your hot buttons."
31. Support customer requirements definition — FAR 15.201 encourages industry input. A well-timed white paper can shape the entire RFP
32. Collaborate with customer on solutions — a 30-minute meeting with the program office before RFP drops is the most valuable meeting in the entire pursuit
33. Complete competitive analysis — research competitors' past performance, pricing history, key personnel, weaknesses
34. Develop win themes (3-4 maximum) — formula: customer benefit + your feature + quantified proof. Example: "Fort Meade's IT operations gain 99.9% uptime through our proven NOC methodology, demonstrated across 3 DoD contracts over 8 years."
35. Develop discriminators — must be specific, verifiable, and address something the customer cares about. "A discriminator without proof is just a claim."
36. Build ghosting strategy — highlight your strength where competitor is weak without naming them. "Our methodology has delivered 100% of milestones on time across 3 comparable contracts" (when incumbent has delivery delays)
37. Determine price-to-win — pull incumbent's contract value from FPDS, estimate their cost structure, determine if you can deliver at 5-10% below while maintaining margin
38. Secure written teaming commitments — "Get the teaming agreement signed before the RFP drops. Not a handshake — a signed document."
39. Conduct Blue Team review — have trusted advisors challenge your capture plan
40. Make Preliminary Bid decision — "If honest pWin is below 30%, think hard about whether to continue."

**Gate: Preliminary Bid Decision** — is our competitive position strong enough to justify proposal investment?

## Phase 4 — Proposal Planning (Steps 41-59)

**Purpose:** Turn the RFP into a writing plan.

Key steps:
41. Analyze RFP within 48 hours — read in order: A → L → M → C → B
42. Verify terms match expectations — if 80% looks familiar, capture homework paid off
43. Check for competitor influence — language mirroring a specific competitor's methodology is a red flag
44. Build compliance matrix — extract every "shall" from C, every instruction from L, every factor from M. Map them. This is the single most important proposal tool
45. Develop proposal outline mirroring Section L structure
46. Create proposal schedule working backwards from due date
47. Assign sections to writers with deadlines and page targets
48. Create writer's packages — win themes, hot buttons, evaluation criteria, page limits for each writer
49. Prepare customer profile summary — one page: agency, program, CO name, top 3 concerns, last contract history
50. Develop theme statements and assign to sections — each theme appears in technical, management, AND past performance from different angles
51. Build bidder comparison matrix scored against Section M factors
52. Create/update solution document
53. Establish file management — one folder, one naming convention, one version manager
54. Draft executive summary early — forces strategic clarity
55. Update price-to-win with actual RFP CLIN structure — LPTA vs. best value changes everything
56. Confirm team availability for proposal period
57. Define style guide per Section L — formatting violations can get you thrown out
58. Conduct kickoff meeting — "Even if your 'team' is three people on a Zoom call, do a kickoff. 30 minutes."
59. Make Bid Validation decision — last off-ramp before full B&P commitment

**Gate: Bid Validation Decision** — are RFP terms as anticipated? Is our solution verified? Is the team identified?

## Phase 5 — Proposal Development (Steps 60-82)

**Purpose:** Write, review, and submit.

Key writing steps (60-71):
- Write to the compliance matrix — every session starts by opening matrix rows for that section
- Lead with benefits, not features — "Every time you write 'Our company has...' stop and rewrite as 'The government gains...'"
- Apply 2:1 customer focus rule
- Use the RFP's own language — don't get creative with their terminology
- Write simple, declarative sentences — subject, verb, object
- Quantify every claim with evidence
- Eliminate weak/hedging language
- Weave win themes throughout
- Develop original graphics with action captions
- Respond in the same order as the RFP
- Explicitly connect each response to the requirement

Review cycle (72-80):
- **Pink Team (~30-40%)** — direction check. "Are we answering the right questions? Are themes showing up?"
- Revise based on Pink Team — compliance first, strategy gaps second, writing quality third
- Continue developing all sections
- **Red Team (~80-90%)** — simulated government evaluation using Section M criteria
- Revise based on Red Team — deficiencies first (fatal), weaknesses second (scoring), minor issues last
- Finalize cost volume — CLINs, labor categories, rates must match technical approach exactly
- Verify cross-volume consistency — org chart matches staffing, staffing matches cost, schedule matches technical
- Final editing and desktop publishing — verify formatting per Section L
- **Gold Team (final)** — "Would I buy from this company?"
- Make Proposal Submittal decision

Submission (82):
- Follow all instructions exactly — portal, naming, format, deadline
- "Submit 24 hours early if you can. Systems crash. Emails bounce. FedConnect goes down at 4:59 PM on due date."

## Phase 6 — Post-Submittal (Steps 83-90)

Key steps:
83. Respond to Evaluation Notices precisely — answer only what was asked, don't volunteer new information
84. Support discussions/orals if requested — rehearse with realistic questions
85. Revalidate pricing for negotiations — know your floor before walking in
86. Prepare negotiating position — decide concessions in advance
87. Make Final Offer decision — if the math doesn't work at the negotiated price, it's okay to walk away
88. Request and attend debrief — ALWAYS, win or lose. "If you lost, you learn exactly why. If you won, you learn what almost cost you."
89. Capture lessons learned — 30 minutes documenting what went right, wrong, and differently. By proposal #5, you'll have a playbook
90. Transition to contract performance — start planning before official notice if confident

## POWeR Writing Process (Steps 91-96)

Schedule allocation:
- **25% Planning and Organizing** — compliance matrix, outline, writer packages, themes
- **50% Writing** — disciplined, matrix-guided writing
- **25% Examining and Revising** — Pink, Red, Gold reviews and edits

Writing mechanics:
- **BLUF** — state your conclusion first, then support it
- **Customer before company** in every sentence — "Fort Meade's operations will benefit from our..." not "Our company will provide Fort Meade with..."
- **4Cs self-check** — Compelling? Clear? Correct? Concise? Four passes, five minutes, huge improvement
