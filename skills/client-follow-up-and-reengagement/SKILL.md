# Client Follow-up and Reengagement

Reconnecting with a client who's gone quiet works if the email reads like someone remembered their specific project, not like a retention campaign. The goal is to get them back into advising — and that depends entirely on whether the first line sounds personal or automated.

## Start from what's on record

Pull from whatever client history is available — a Neoserra report, an uploaded client file, or details given in chat. Before drafting, note:

- what services or advising sessions the client already used
- any business idea, goal, or aspiration they mentioned
- anything that's changed since — a program update, a new resource, general progress — that gives a real reason to meet again

Weave these in as specific references, not a recap of their file. "I remember you were exploring the catering side of your bakery idea" gives someone a reason to reply; a summary of their intake form doesn't.

## No client history? Write it warm anyway

If no report or details are available, don't stall waiting for them, and don't let the email read as generic just because the input was thin. Default to warm, inviting language that works for any pre-venture client — genuine interest in how their idea is coming along, an open invitation to catch up.

## Always ask for the meeting

Every email closes with an invitation to schedule time, framed as being for the client's benefit — their next step, their progress — not for closing out a file. Getting them back on the calendar is the actual point of the email, not a friendly note on its own.

## If the reply surfaces an accomplishment, record it

The source GPT named "data collection regarding SBDC Milestones" as a goal but never said what to collect or how, so this section is the minimum honest reading of that intent rather than a procedure Brandon wrote.

When the client history you're working from — or the client's reply — shows something they actually completed since going quiet, match it against `references/neoserra-milestones.md` and note it separately from the email draft, using that file's exact category wording. Neoserra reporting keys off those names, so a paraphrase does not register. Hand it to the advisor as a short "worth logging" line; do not put milestone language into the email itself, which would make a warm note read like file maintenance.

## Voice

Warm, professional, and supportive — business insight delivered with a personal touch. Avoid cold or transactional phrasing ("following up on your file," "per our records"); it reads as automated, which is exactly what fills the rest of the client's inbox. Write like someone who remembers their entrepreneurial journey, not like someone closing a ticket.
