# VOSBTC26 Workshop Rubric
**Session:** Using AI for Business Growth — established veteran-owned small businesses
**Format:** 60-minute conference workshop
**Purpose:** Score the session design the way a curriculum/workshop designer would before it goes in front of a room. Use it on the outline now, on the deck at draft, and on the dry run.

## How to score

Each criterion gets a 1–4 score. Multiply by the weight. 100 points possible.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 4 — Conference-ready | A designer would put this on the main stage as-is. |
| 3 — Solid | Works; one specific fix would make it a 4. |
| 2 — Needs work | The idea is there but the room will feel the gap. |
| 1 — Missing | Not designed yet, or designed for a different audience. |

**Ship threshold:** 80+ overall, and no criterion scored 1. A single 1 in Audience Fit, Demos, or Time Design sends the session back regardless of total.

## Criteria

### 1. Audience fit — established veteran-owned businesses (weight 4)

The VBOC partner insight is the design constraint: these owners are past startup. They have processes, staff, customers, and no patience for "what is ChatGPT."

| 4 | Every example is a business that already runs (payroll, contracts, repeat customers). Language assumes competence. No 101 content. Veteran context shows up in substance (GovCon, VOSB/SDVOSB certification workflows, VA, capability statements) not just in the title slide. |
|---|---|
| 3 | Examples fit established businesses; veteran-specific angle is thin or decorative. |
| 2 | Mix of startup and established examples; some intro-level explanation of AI tools. |
| 1 | Generic "AI for small business" talk with the audience name swapped in. |

**Evidence to look for:** Named business types in the examples. Zero slides defining what an LLM is. At least one veteran-specific use case (certification package, SAM.gov, past-performance narrative, VA vendor portal).

### 2. One clear promise (weight 3)

A 60-minute session can deliver one transformation, not a survey.

| 4 | The session promise fits in one sentence an attendee could repeat afterward (e.g., "Leave with one process in your business mapped and one AI-assisted version of it you tried today"). Everything on the agenda serves that sentence. |
|---|---|
| 3 | Clear promise; one agenda block doesn't serve it. |
| 2 | Two or three competing promises. |
| 1 | Promise is a topic ("AI for growth"), not an outcome. |

**Evidence:** Write the promise at the top of the outline. Cut anything that doesn't earn its place under it.

### 3. Process-first framing (weight 3)

Per the VPOC discussion, effective use comes from understanding your own internal processes first, then applying AI. The session should teach the sequence, not the tool.

| 4 | Session opens with the owner's process, not the tool. There is an explicit "map it, then automate it" model, and attendees apply it to their own business during the hour. |
|---|---|
| 3 | Process framing is present but comes after the tool demos. |
| 2 | Mentioned as a principle, never operationalized. |
| 1 | Tool-first: here are five AI apps you should try. |

**Evidence:** A worksheet or slide where the attendee names one recurring process (quoting, invoicing follow-up, proposal drafting, scheduling) before any AI is shown.

### 4. Demo-to-talk ratio (weight 4)

Practical and demo-heavy, no theory without application. This is the criterion most workshops fail.

| 4 | At least 50% of the hour is live demo or hands-on. Every concept is shown before it is explained. Demos use realistic inputs from a plausible veteran-owned business, not lorem ipsum. |
|---|---|
| 3 | 30–50% demo. Concepts sometimes explained before shown. |
| 2 | One or two demos bolted on to a slide talk. |
| 1 | Slides only, or demos are screenshots. |

**Evidence:** Minute-by-minute run of show with demo blocks highlighted. Actual sample inputs prepared and saved. Backup recordings for every live demo in case Wi-Fi dies.

### 5. Grounded in real SBDC advising data (weight 3)

Two years of SBDC notes and experience should show up as specifics, not as "in my experience."

| 4 | Three or more anonymized, concrete client patterns (what they tried, what worked, what it saved in hours or dollars). At least one honest failure or misuse example. Any statistics cited have a source on the slide. |
|---|---|
| 3 | Two concrete patterns; no failure example. |
| 2 | Anecdotes without numbers or outcomes. |
| 1 | No advising data used. |

**Evidence:** A source list. Neoserra-derived patterns pulled and anonymized before the deck is built.

### 6. Time design for 60 minutes (weight 3)

| 4 | Opening hook under 3 minutes. No content block longer than 12 minutes without an audience action. Hard stop at 50 minutes of content, leaving 10 for Q&A and the close. Buffer identified: one block marked "cut if behind." |
|---|---|
| 3 | Timed agenda exists; one block runs long or there's no cut plan. |
| 2 | Agenda has topics but no minutes attached. |
| 1 | No timing. |

**Evidence:** Run of show with clock times. The "cut if behind" block named.

### 7. Engagement and interaction design (weight 2)

Adult learners in a conference room retain what they do, not what they hear.

| 4 | At least three attendee actions: a write-it-down moment, a pair/table share, and one thing they try on their own phone or laptop. Questions are planned, not "any questions?" |
|---|---|
| 3 | Two planned interactions. |
| 2 | One interaction, usually at the end. |
| 1 | Lecture. |

**Evidence:** Interactions written into the run of show with the exact prompt the presenter will say.

### 8. Implementation path after the room (weight 3)

The VPOC point was two-part: understand your process, then implement efficiently. The session has to set up the second half.

| 4 | Attendees leave with a one-page next-step sheet: the process they picked, the first AI-assisted step, a 30-day checkpoint, and the specific free resource to use (SBDC appointment link, a named tool, a template). SBDC follow-up appointment offered with a concrete way to book it. |
|---|---|
| 3 | Takeaway exists but is generic (a list of tools). |
| 2 | "Reach out to the SBDC" without a mechanism. |
| 1 | No after-session path. |

**Evidence:** The handout is drafted. The booking link or QR code is on the last slide.

### 9. Risk, cost, and trust handled honestly (weight 2)

Established owners will ask about data privacy, client confidentiality, GovCon/CUI restrictions, hallucinations, and what it costs. If the session doesn't raise it first, the first question will.

| 4 | One short, plain-language block on what not to put into AI tools (client PII, contract CUI, financials without a business account), what it realistically costs per month, and how to check AI output. Delivered as rules of thumb, not a compliance lecture. |
|---|---|
| 3 | Covered, but as a caveat slide at the end. |
| 2 | Only mentioned if asked. |
| 1 | Not addressed. |

**Evidence:** A "don't paste this" list on a slide. Cost figures current as of the month of the session.

### 10. Materials and accessibility (weight 2)

| 4 | Slides readable from the back row: minimum 28pt body, high contrast, one idea per slide. Handout available as a printed sheet and a QR link. Demos zoomed so text is legible on a projector. Presenter has a no-internet fallback. |
|---|---|
| 3 | Slides are good; handout or fallback is missing. |
| 2 | Dense slides, small text. |
| 1 | Materials not built. |

**Evidence:** Test the deck on a projector or at 50% zoom. Print the handout once.

## Ten-minute pre-flight (use the morning of)

Answer yes/no. Any "no" is the thing to fix first.

- Can I say the session promise in one sentence without looking?
- Does the first demo happen before minute 8?
- Is every demo input saved locally, with a recorded backup?
- Do I have three real (anonymized) client stories with an outcome attached?
- Is there a block I've already decided to cut if I'm behind at minute 30?
- Is the "don't paste this into AI" slide in the deck?
- Is the booking link / QR on the last slide and on the handout?
- Can the back row read the smallest text on the busiest slide?

---

## Review cadence

Score three times: outline (aim for 60+, no 1s), draft deck (aim for 75+), dry run (aim for 80+). Track scores in the table above so the trend is visible.
